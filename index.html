About

The Concept

Inspired by Borges' "The Library of Babel" (1941) and libraryofbabel.info, the Playlist of Babel is the auditory equivalent — a library containing every possible one-second sound.

Every melody. Every word in every language. Every birdsong, thunderclap, and note of every instrument. The opening second of every song ever recorded, and every song that could be. They all exist here at a unique address.

Most are noise — meaningless static, just as most pages in the Library of Babel are gibberish. But somewhere in the vastness is every beautiful sound that has ever been or could ever be.

How It Works

Each track is 1 second of 8-bit mono audio at 8,000 Hz. That's 8,000 bytes — 8,000 amplitude values between 0 and 255, totaling 64,000 bits.

The address of a track is the base64url encoding of its raw audio data — a unique 10,667-character string. No server, no database. Audio is generated directly from the address in your browser.

Every combination of 64,000 bits is a valid track. Every track has exactly one address. Every address maps to exactly one track. 264,000 total tracks.

Browsing & Search

Browse — Navigate by choosing prefix characters. Each narrows the space by 64×. A "playlist" is all tracks sharing a prefix.

Search — Record audio or upload a file, select any 1-second region, and it gets resampled to 8 kHz, quantized to 8-bit, and encoded to its exact address.

Random — Pull a random address and hear what the void sounds like.
